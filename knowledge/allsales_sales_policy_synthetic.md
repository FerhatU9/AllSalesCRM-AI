# AllSales CRM — Sales Lead Qualification & Follow-Up Handbook

**Training document — entirely synthetic.** AllSales CRM, its rules, numbers and procedures are fictional. This material was created solely for an educational RAG assignment. It is not legal advice, a representation of any actual company policy, or a statement of Dutch/EU legal requirements.

**Version:** 1.0  | **Effective for the example:** 1 October 2026 | **Document owner:** Sales Operations (fictional)

## Section 1 — Business scope and intended audience

AllSales CRM is a fictional business-to-business software company selling a customer relationship management tool. Its sales staff use this handbook to decide which prospective customers to contact, how quickly to respond, and when to involve colleagues. The normal target market is companies with **10 to 500 employees** located in the **European Union or the United Kingdom**. The preferred industries are **software-as-a-service (SaaS), professional services, and retail**. Sales representatives may not promise product features that are not described in approved product documentation.

Organizations outside the EU and UK are **not automatically rejected**: send their lead record to a sales manager for market-eligibility review. Organizations with more than 500 employees also go to an enterprise manager for special review rather than being automatically rejected. Organizations with fewer than 10 employees normally enter the nurture list rather than the active sales queue.

## Section 2 — Required lead record

Before a lead can be qualified, its customer relationship management (CRM) record must have the **company name, country, employee count, industry, contact's name, business email address, stated use case, lead source, consent status, and assigned owner**. The owner records the most recent contact date after outreach. If either the **country** or the **business email address** is missing, mark the lead **Incomplete**; do not assign a priority tier until the missing item is supplied. All remaining mandatory fields must also be completed before the lead is moved into an active sales queue.

Use the CRM only for information needed to communicate with the business contact. Do not store passwords, bank card details, passports, private medical information, or other unnecessary sensitive details in the lead record.

## Section 3 — Lead scoring rules

The lead score is the sum of four separate factors and has a maximum of **100 points**:

- **Company fit (0, 20, or 40 points):** award 40 if the organization is within 10–500 employees, is in the EU or UK, and operates in SaaS, professional services, or retail. Award 20 if it satisfies exactly two of those three criteria. Otherwise award 0.
- **Purchase urgency (0, 10, or 25 points):** award 25 if the customer wants a decision within 30 calendar days; award 10 for 31–90 days; otherwise award 0.
- **Decision authority (0, 10, or 20 points):** award 20 if the contact controls the budget or has final approval; award 10 if the contact influences the decision but cannot approve it; otherwise award 0.
- **Engagement (0, 5, or 15 points):** award 15 if a product demonstration has been booked; award 5 if the contact has responded to an outreach email but has not booked a demonstration; otherwise award 0.

For an incomplete record, do not use the numeric score to override the Incomplete status. For unusual organizations outside the standard market, apply the market review rules in Section 1 in addition to calculating the score.

## Section 4 — Priority tiers and follow-up times

- **Priority A: 75–100 points.** A sales representative should make the first personalized contact within **one business day** of qualification.
- **Priority B: 50–74 points.** A sales representative should make the first personalized contact within **three business days** of qualification.
- **Priority C: 0–49 points.** Add the lead to the nurture queue and schedule a new review after **30 calendar days**. Do not treat a Priority C lead as an urgent sales opportunity.

These times are internal example targets. A **business day** means Monday–Friday, excluding any company holidays recorded in the fictional internal calendar. Where an urgent safety or security event is involved, Section 8 takes priority over ordinary sales response targets.

## Section 5 — Sales team routing

For businesses in the EU or UK with **10–200 employees**, route the lead to the **SMB Sales Team**. For businesses with **201–500 employees**, route it to the **Enterprise Sales Team**. Leads with more than 500 employees are sent to the **Enterprise Sales Manager** for special review. Region is recorded as **EU** or **UK** to assign the appropriate territory owner. Leads located outside both regions go to the sales manager for eligibility review, regardless of score.

Sales team routing is separate from priority scoring. For example, a 250-employee UK business can be sent to Enterprise Sales but still hold Priority B if its score is 65.

## Section 6 — Product demonstrations, pilot periods, and discounts

A sales representative can offer a standard **14-calendar-day product pilot**, subject to the usual product capacity checks. The representative may propose a discount of **up to 10%** without manager approval. **Any discount greater than 10% requires written approval from a sales manager before it is offered**. If a prospective customer asks for custom contract terms, refer the request to the sales manager; do not invent a contractual commitment.

## Section 7 — Consent and information handling

Record whether the business contact has agreed to receive marketing material as **Opted In, Opted Out, or Unknown**. If the contact is marked **Opted Out**, do not send marketing campaigns. An Unknown status must not be represented as permission to receive marketing. For the example organization's internal housekeeping procedure, inactive lead records are flagged for a data review after **90 calendar days** without contact. This synthetic policy is not a summary of GDPR or any other law.

## Section 8 — Escalation and exceptions

If a lead reports a **suspected security incident**, forward the report to the **Security Team on the same business day**. If an existing customer reports an **urgent service outage**, escalate to **Customer Support within four business hours**. Do not promise that these teams will resolve the issue within that period; those timings concern internal escalation only.

For unclear scoring factors, record the uncertainty and ask the sales manager before treating the lead as Priority A. Do not make up details that are missing from the handbook, including specific software subscription prices or the company's physical office address.

## Section 9 — How to answer policy questions

When a team member asks how the rules apply, state the relevant requirement, apply any available numerical rules, and identify the handbook section. If the handbook does not contain an answer, say **“The handbook does not specify this information.”** Avoid guessing. Escalate exceptions as directed in the relevant section.
